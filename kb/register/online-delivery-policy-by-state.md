# Online Delivery Policy by State (White Card)

Which states permit the White Card (construction induction, **CPCWHS1001**) to be completed online, and in what mode. This is a **compliance-critical** reference: never advertise self-paced online White Card to residents of a state that does not permit it — that was the exact loophole regulators closed in 2019.

**Verified:** 26 May 2026 against multiple 2026 RTO/industry guides. **NSW re-verified 1 August 2026** against the regulator's own primary documents, and the previous NSW position was wrong — see §2A. **QLD re-verified 2 August 2026** against WHSQ's conditions of agreement and the Queensland Training Ombudsman's review — the ✅ was right, its stated reason was wrong, see §2C. **Re-verify:** annually and whenever a state WHS regulator changes its delivery rules. Confirm the actual delivery mode with the RTO partner before wording any course page.

> ⚠️ **Provenance warning, updated 2 Aug 2026.** **NSW (§2A) and QLD (§2C) now rest on regulator documents.** Every other row in §2 — **WA, TAS, ACT, VIC, SA, NT** — still rests on the industry-guide sourcing described in §4, not on a regulator document. Industry guides are not a regulator. Of the two rows since checked against primary sources, one was **wrong in the permissive direction** (NSW) and one was **right for the wrong reason** (QLD). Treat the unchecked rows as UNVERIFIED for publishing purposes and read the regulator before any page repeats them. **TAS and ACT are the next two**, because both back live or planned pages.

---

## 1. The core rule (post-2019)

**Self-paced, fully online** White Card is permitted **only for WA residents and TAS residents** (since 2019). Residents of every other state/territory must complete the training **face-to-face**. The card is **nationally recognised** once issued, regardless of where or how it was completed — but the *delivery* rules are set by the resident's state.

**"Face-to-face" does not automatically include the virtual classroom.** Some regulators accept trainer-led real-time video delivery as satisfying the face-to-face requirement and some do not, and this is settled per jurisdiction by that regulator's own conditions, never by inference from the phrase. **SafeWork NSW's published conditions do not provide for it** (§2A) — and ABE's NSW course is nonetheless delivered that way by its RTO under a recorded exemption (**§2A-1**, closed 2 Aug 2026). Do not carry an acceptance from one state to another.

A supporting reason self-paced delivery is restricted: since December 2016 the assessment includes a **PPE demonstration** component (the learner must correctly fit and wear common construction PPE — see `ppe-requirements.md`), which regulators want supervised in real time.

### The two documents, and how they interlock

SafeWork NSW splits RTO conditions into **General Conditions** (all authorisation regimes) and **Specific Conditions** (per regime). The General Conditions define Specific Conditions as "The conditions for RTOs delivering services in NSW for a specified WHS authorisation regime."

**1. General Conditions** — [General conditions for RTOs delivering training and assessment](https://www.safework.nsw.gov.au/resource-library/licence-and-registrations/general-conditions-for-registered-training-organisations-delivering-training-and-assessment), HTML, "Effective 1 October 2022", framed under the **WHS Regulation 2025 (NSW)**. Read in a browser 1 Aug 2026.

- §4.3.1: "Face-to-face delivery is the preferred training delivery mode. **Other training delivery modes, including Connected delivery, can be used if provided for in the relevant Specific Conditions.**"
- Definitions: **Face-to-face** = "Services delivery where the trainer and learner are **physically located together**, with the training delivered live and in real-time". **Connected delivery** = "Services delivery by live video streaming/conferencing, using platforms such as Skype, Microsoft Teams or Zoom" with real-time EOI, interaction and direct-observation assessment.

So Connected delivery (the trainer-led virtual classroom) is a **permission that the Specific Conditions must grant**. It is not a form of face-to-face; the General Conditions define the two as distinct modes.

**2. Specific Conditions for GIT** — "Conditions for Registered Training Organisations Delivering General Construction Induction Training in NSW under the Work Health and Safety Regulation 2017", catalogue **SW08319**, dated September 2022, **effective from 1 October 2022**. [PDF](https://www.safework.nsw.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0006/51846/conditions-for-RTOs-delivering-general-construction-induction-training-oct-2022.pdf), still the document linked from the live [General construction induction RTOs](https://www.safework.nsw.gov.au/licences-and-registrations/registered-training-organisations/construction-induction-rtos) page on 1 Aug 2026. Text extracted from the signed PDF and read 1 Aug 2026.

- **Clause 1(q)** — the operative sentence: the RTO must "Ensure all training delivered in NSW is delivered by a nominated trainer using **face-to-face delivery techniques**. **Distance education and on-line learning are not permitted in NSW for the delivery of GIT.**"
- **Clause 14**: "RTOs must ensure the duration of GIT training is **not less than six hours** (excluding breaks and administrative activities such as registration or EOI checks)." Clause 16 repeats that breaks are excluded from the "minimum 6 hour" requirement. **This is the source for the six-hour figure, which the register previously carried unsourced.**

### The conclusion

**The GIT Specific Conditions do not provide for Connected delivery.** Under General Conditions §4.3.1 that is exactly the condition on which Connected delivery would be permitted, so **trainer-led virtual classroom delivery is not authorised for GIT in NSW** on the regulator's published conditions. The previous register row, and `kb/rules/authority-model.md`'s matching line, asserted the reverse.

### What this does not settle, stated plainly

- **The Regulation-year mismatch is real.** The GIT conditions are framed under the **WHS Regulation 2017**; the General Conditions are now framed under the **WHS Regulation 2025**. The GIT document may simply be overdue for reissue. But a stale document is not a permissive one: nothing published grants Connected delivery for GIT, and an absent permission cannot be read as a granted one.
- **No exemption route is published.** The GIT conditions were searched for exemption/variation provisions; the "variation" clauses concern training-session notifications and the "special arrangements" concern evidence of identity only. Neither touches delivery mode. The General Conditions' record-keeping clause does reference "approvals from the Regulator for all exemptions to these conditions", so an **RTO-specific** exemption is conceivable, but nothing public evidences one.
- **Two routes would put a public source behind the mode** — (a) SafeWork NSW confirming in writing that Connected delivery is provided for, or (b) Upskill Institute holding a specific SafeWork NSW approval or exemption for video delivery — and **neither can be verified from public sources.** They are recorded here for completeness only. **Neither is an open action, and neither is a precondition for the page.** §2A-1 settled the mode as a recorded exemption on 2 Aug 2026; if a written confirmation ever arrives unprompted it would upgrade the sourcing, nothing more. Do not chase it, do not file it, do not raise it.

### Standing on the earlier evidence

The 1 Aug 2026 build run cited two facts in support of the virtual-classroom position; neither survives as support, and both should stop being offered as such:

- *"Upskill holds SafeWork NSW registration RTO800520 for GIT."* True, and irrelevant to mode: the registration authorises delivery, and the conditions govern how. Every registered GIT RTO is bound by clause 1(q).
- *"A regulator listing a provider whose advertised offering is by Zoom is evidence."* It is evidence of what the provider advertises, not of what the regulator permits. On the published conditions it is as consistent with an advertising non-compliance (which General Conditions §5.1 and §5.4 make auditable) as with a permission.

## 2B. WA — located in WA, not resident in WA (read at source 1 August 2026)

The register said "WA residents" until 1 Aug 2026. **That is not the regulator's test, and the difference decides who can buy the course.**

WorkSafe WA, [Candidates to provide evidence that they are located in Western Australia](https://www.worksafe.wa.gov.au/candidates-provide-evidence-they-are-located-western-australia), read in a browser 1 Aug 2026, states in full:

> "Construction Induction Training cards (CIT) are supplied to an RTO on the condition that the CIT cards are only issued to candidates who provide evidence that they are **located in Western Australia at the time of the assessment** for the CPCWHS1001 Prepare to work safely in the construction industry unit of competency."

The page attributes this to "Item 3(a) page 5 of the Terms and Conditions 2022".

**Accepted evidence, as listed by WorkSafe WA:** current WA driver's licence; current WA learner driver's permit; current WA Student Identification; a recent utilities invoice with a WA address in the candidate's name; WA Construction Industry Training Fund payment eligibility; or a **WA postal address to which the CIT card is to be posted**.

**Why the wording matters.** The last item alone shows this is a presence-and-nexus test, not a residency test. Someone working in WA on a fly-in-fly-out or temporary basis, with a WA postal address, satisfies it; the word "resident" would wrongly turn them away, and it would equally wrongly imply that a WA resident sitting the assessment interstate qualifies. Use the regulator's wording on pages: **located in WA at the time of assessment**.

**Unit code note.** This page names the unit **CPCWHS1001** (single C), matching the house rule in CLAUDE.md. WorkSafe WA's own [Construction induction training](https://www.worksafe.wa.gov.au/construction-induction-training) page, in its replacement-card section, says "CPCCWHS1001" (double C) — an inconsistency **on the regulator's site**, not in this repo. The repo's position is unchanged and correct; recorded here only so the next run that spots it does not treat it as a defect to fix.

## 2C. QLD — CRTD is face-to-face, and it is approved per RTO (read at source 2 August 2026)

The register said "✅ real-time · Virtual classroom available to remote QLD residents", sourced to
industry guides. **The ✅ is right. The reason was wrong, and the remoteness gate does not exist.**

### The operative document

**Conditions of agreement for registered training organisations to issue general construction
induction training cards in Queensland**, made under **s.325 of the Work Health and Safety Regulation
2011**, **Version 6.1, effective 25/07/2023**. Read via `pdftotext` on 2 Aug 2026.
[PDF](https://trainingombudsman.qld.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Attachment-4-GCIT-Conditions-V6.1.pdf)

⚠️ **WHSQ names this document but does not link it.** Its
[RTO page](https://www.worksafe.qld.gov.au/licensing-and-registrations/work-health-and-safety-training/registered-training-organisations/general-construction-induction-information-for-registered-training-organisations)
(last updated 29 Sep 2017) says RTOs "must comply with the Conditions of agreement…" as plain text
with no hyperlink. The copy read is the one published by the **Queensland Training Ombudsman** as
Attachment 4 to its May 2024 review. If a newer version than 6.1 exists, it was not findable publicly
on 2 Aug 2026 — obtain it from `GCIT@oir.qld.gov.au` before relying on any clause below changing.

### QLD is the mirror image of NSW, and this is the whole finding

Definitions, quoted in full:

> "**Connected real time delivery (CRTD) is a form of face-to-face training and assessment:** using
> live video streaming/conferencing on platforms such as Zoom, Skype or Teams · involving real time
> interaction between a nominated trainer and students · involving active participation by students ·
> where a student's evidence of identity can be verified in real time · where direct observation or
> verbal assessment can be undertaken for all assessment components."

> "**General Construction Induction Training (GCIT)** means the GCIT course delivered face-to-face
> **either in a classroom or by CRTD**, for the purpose of issuing a GCIT card."

**In NSW the regulator defines face-to-face and connected delivery as separate modes, so the virtual
classroom needed a permission never granted (§2A). In QLD the regulator defines CRTD as a species of
face-to-face.** Do not carry either state's reasoning to the other. The phrase "face-to-face" means
different things in the two documents.

### But it is gated per RTO, and the gate is real

WHSQ's own announcement is the conditional: the November 2022 conditions "allow GCIT to be delivered
face-to-face via connected real time delivery (CRTD) **where an RTO is approved to do so**"
([eSafe Construction, Nov 2022](https://www.worksafe.qld.gov.au/news-and-events/newsletters/esafe-newsletters/esafe-editions/esafe-construction/november-2022/new-conditions-for-white-cards-in-queensland);
a copy is in `new site/reference/`).

**V6.1 contains no clause describing that approval**, and reading only V6.1 would suggest CRTD is open
to any approved GCIT RTO. It is not. The Training Ombudsman's review documents the process: an RTO
"will not be approved to deliver the training via CRTD without first being approved to deliver the
training face-to-face", and must then complete a **two-step application** — Step One, documentation
(platform, CRTD training and assessment strategy, evidence-of-identity process, technical support);
**Step Two, a simulated CRTD session with a WHSQ Inspector**, after which it is approved or refused.

> **Method note, recorded because it nearly went the other way.** Absence of the approval clause from
> the conditions document is not absence of the requirement. This is the §2A lesson inverted: there, a
> permission was quoted without its condition; here, the condition sits in a different instrument
> entirely. Two documents, one rule.

### Which RTOs hold it — and Blue Dog does

**13 of 226** approved GCIT RTOs currently hold CRTD approval. **Blue Dog Training Pty Ltd (RTO
31193), ABE Education's QLD White Card partner, is one of them — approved 7 June 2020, current.**

Two independent government sources agree:
1. The Ombudsman review names "Blue Dog Training" in its list of RTOs approved for CRTD as at
   30 September 2023 (10 RTOs at that date).
2. WHSQ's published
   [list of approved RTOs (XLSX)](https://www.worksafe.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/excel_doc/0025/21589/rto-approved-general-construction-induction-training.xlsx),
   file last saved **10 July 2025**, carries a current CRTD approval for RTO 31193 with no termination.

⚠️ **How that was read from the XLSX, because the obvious column lies.** The human-readable column
*"Approved to deliver via connected real time delivery (CRTD)"* is an unresolved external-link formula
and renders **blank for every RTO in the file**, including ones the Ombudsman confirms are approved.
**Its blankness is not evidence of anything.** The status lives in ten hidden date columns
(application received / in review / withdrawn / refused / **current-approved** / terminated ×4), where
a date means the event occurred and `1` means it did not; the "Max date" column is the current status.
Deriving the approved set that way yields 13, of which 8 match the Ombudsman's 10 by name and the
3 extras all carry approval dates *after* September 2023. The method cross-validates, but this is a
**derivation, not a printed "Yes"** — if a page is ever challenged on it, confirm with Blue Dog or
`GCIT@oir.qld.gov.au`.

### The conditions a QLD page must respect

| Cond. | Requirement |
|---|---|
| 36 | The RTO must use the **assessment provided by the Regulator** — mandated, not the RTO's own |
| 37 | "The RTO must only deliver GCIT to students **physically located in Queensland**" — a location test, as in WA (§2B). **Not** residency, **not** remoteness |
| 38 | Eye protection, hearing protection, hard hat, high-visibility vest/shirt/jacket and a copy of the *WHS Act 2011* (Qld) must be **present and available to students during assessment** — for CRTD that means the learner needs them wherever they are sitting |
| 39 | Maximum student-to-trainer ratio **15:1** |
| 40 | Minimum training delivery duration **four and a half hours**, excluding administration and rest breaks |
| 41 | If delivered cumulatively, completed **within three months** |
| 42 | CRTD must **not** include a pre-training requirement, **self-paced learning**, or pre-recorded trainer videos or teaching content (educational videos illustrating construction sites are excluded from the ban) |
| 43 | The RTO must give the Regulator a link or invitation to join a CRTD session on request |
| 32 | The RTO must notify WHSQ of every session **at least 24 hours** beforehand, including the delivery method (classroom or CRTD) |
| 34 | Students must be **13 years of age or over** |

### Two traps for the next session

1. **WHSQ's worker-facing page contradicts its own conditions.**
   [General construction induction](https://www.worksafe.qld.gov.au/licensing-and-registrations/work-health-and-safety-licences/what-licence-do-i-need/general-construction-induction),
   last updated **4 October 2022**, says under "Where can I do my training?" only: *"You need to
   complete face-to-face training with a Registered Training Organisation (RTO)."* It never mentions
   CRTD. It predates the November 2022 conditions that introduced CRTD and the V6.1 conditions of
   July 2023. **It is stale and simplified, not a reversal.** A future session will find it and try to
   turn this row back to in-person-only. It should not. Read V6.1 §Definitions instead.
2. **QLD accepts both unit codes.** V6.1: *"GCIT course means 'CPCCWHS1001 Prepare to work safely in
   the construction industry' or 'CPCWHS1001 Prepare to work safely in the construction industry'."*
   WHSQ's public pages name only **CPCCWHS1001** (double C), the code CLAUDE.md treats as superseded
   and `check-claims` §6 fails on inside `src/`. The house rule is right nationally and is unchanged;
   a QLD page must neither present CPCCWHS1001 as the current national code nor "correct" the
   regulator's own wording when quoting it. Same shape as the WA note in §2B.

**The 2019 rural exception is gone.** From 1 Feb 2019 all QLD training had to be face-to-face, "with
an exception for Queensland residents who lived in a rural area more than 100kms from an RTO approved
to deliver the course" (Ombudsman review, history section). **That arrangement was superseded by the
November 2022 CRTD regime.** CRTD is open to anyone physically located in Queensland, from a
CRTD-approved RTO — there is no remoteness or distance test. The register's previous note said the
opposite and was the industry-guide claim, not the regulator's.

## 3. What this means for ABE pages (apply this)

ABE offers White Card in **QLD, WA, TAS** (RTO partner Blue Dog Training, RTO 31193), **ACT** (AlertForce, RTO 91826) and **NSW** (Upskill Institute, RTO 45708).

- **WA and TAS pages** may advertise genuine **self-paced online** completion, subject to the state test. **WA: screen for the candidate being located in WA at the time of assessment** (§2B) and use that wording, not "WA residents". TAS likewise screens by state.
- **QLD, ACT and NSW pages must NOT advertise self-paced online White Card** to residents of those states. For ACT, AlertForce delivers face-to-face in a classroom (in person).
- **QLD: a fully online page is publishable, and this is the one row where the regulator helps rather than constrains.** WHSQ defines CRTD **as** face-to-face (§2C), and Blue Dog Training (RTO 31193) holds CRTD approval, so a QLD page may say the course is completed online with a live trainer. Four things it must carry: the student must be **physically located in Queensland** (not "resident"), the session runs a **minimum of four and a half hours**, **nothing is self-paced or pre-recorded**, and the learner must have **PPE to hand for the assessment** — eye and hearing protection, hard hat, high-visibility clothing. Do **not** claim "approved by WHSQ" of ABE Education: the CRTD approval is **Blue Dog's**, and attributing it to ABE is the same error as the NSW attribution in §2A-1.
- **NSW: settled, not a blocker.** ABE's NSW White Card is delivered by Upskill Institute (RTO 45708) as a trainer-led virtual classroom, recorded as an exemption on 2 Aug 2026 (**§2A-1**). The page states the mode as the **RTO's own**, carries **no verification link and no "Verified" badge on that claim**, and never says SafeWork NSW accepts live video as face-to-face. That is the entire rule; §2A-1 has the detail. Do not reopen it.
- **Screen by state of residence** at enrolment/checkout so a resident of a face-to-face state is not sold a self-paced WA/TAS course as a workaround — closing this loophole was the whole point of the 2019 change.
- When in doubt, describe the card as **nationally recognised** (true) but keep **delivery-mode claims state-accurate**.

- 2026 RTO/industry guides (Tradie Training, FirstAidPro, National Courses) cross-checked for the self-paced vs virtual-classroom distinction. **These are not a regulator.** They remain the sole basis for the **WA, TAS, ACT, VIC, SA and NT** rows in §2.
