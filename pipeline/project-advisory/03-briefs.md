# 03 · Shape + section briefs — `/project-advisory`

## 3a · Shape

**No archetype fits — see `01-source-map.md` §A for the check against all ten.** Built on the
reader's own decision order, Recipe C, `Product` schema, no `Course` node, no authority model.

Two Stage 2 findings drive every brief below:
1. **The reader arrives warm, from a course page they already bought from** — not from search. No
   need to explain what a construction budget is.
2. **The competition is free** — so the page's job is not "here is a spreadsheet" but "here is why
   $89 beats the free one you were about to download."

Decision order: what it is → what is actually in it → why not the free one → who it is for (and is
not) → what it costs → how to get it.

## 3b · Section briefs

### Section 1 — What is the Project Advisory Pack?
`id="what"` · marker 01
- **Claim:** three downloadable tools for running the money side of an owner-builder project: a
  budget estimator, a contracts calculator, and a written guide.
- **Reader arrives:** having clicked a cross-link from their state's course page. Knows they are
  building, does not know what this is.
- **Objection defused:** "is this another course?" — it is not, and saying so immediately prevents
  the reader from filing it as more training they have already bought.
- **Facts:** the three components, `01-source-map.md` §C.
- **Distinctive material:** none needed. This section's job is orientation, fast.
- **Carrier:** AnswerCapsule + FactGrid (price / format / what's inside / who it's for)
- **Fails if:** the reader still thinks it is a course, or cannot tell what physically arrives.

### Section 2 — What is actually in it?
`id="inside"` · marker 02
- **Claim:** each of the three components, described by what it does for the reader rather than what
  it is.
- **Reader arrives:** deciding. This is the section that earns or loses the $89.
- **Objection defused:** "is this three PDFs of generic advice?"
- **Facts:** all three components in full, `01-source-map.md` §C. The **20% contingency** and the
  **progress-claim tracking** are the two concrete details that prove it is a working tool.
- **Distinctive material:** Stage 2 §D — the contingency being applied *automatically* is the thing
  no free template does, and under-budgeting is the classic owner-builder failure.
- **Carrier:** TopicGrid, one card per component
- **Fails if:** a reader cannot picture opening the spreadsheet and using it.

### Section 3 — Why not just use a free template?
`id="why-paid"` · marker 03
- **Claim:** free templates give you a static estimate; this follows the money through the build and
  explains the contracts.
- **Reader arrives:** having seen free spreadsheets, reasonably wondering why pay.
- **Objection defused:** "I can get a construction budget spreadsheet for nothing" — **the single
  strongest objection this page faces**, and Stage 2 proved it is what the market is searching for.
- **Facts:** the four gaps in Stage 2 §D.
- **Distinctive material:** this section exists *because* of Stage 2. Without that finding the page
  would not have argued the point at all.
- **Carrier:** CanCant — what the pack does / what a free template leaves you to work out
- **Fails if:** it disparages free tools rather than drawing an honest line. A reader who genuinely
  only needs an estimate should be able to tell that a free one will do.

### Section 4 — Who it is for, and who it is not for
`id="who"` · marker 04
- **Claim:** it suits an owner builder running their own trades and money on a real project; it is
  not for someone whose builder handles all of that, and it is not required by anyone.
- **Reader arrives:** wondering if they are the right buyer.
- **Objection defused:** "am I being sold something I have to have?" — **the hard rule from
  `01-source-map.md` §B lives here**: the pack satisfies no regulatory requirement.
- **Facts:** none regulatory. The obligation facts stay on the course pages where they are sourced.
- **Distinctive material:** telling a reader they may not need this is the same trust move
  `/owner-builder-insurance` makes, and it is available here for the same reason: ABE Education is
  not obliged to sell it.
- **Carrier:** CanCant or Note
- **Fails if:** the page implies the pack is part of the owner-builder requirement. That is the one
  claim that would be a real breach.

### Section 5 — What it costs and how to get it
`id="cost"` · marker 05
- **Claim:** $89.00, one payment, downloadable documents, nothing recurring.
- **Reader arrives:** ready, wanting no surprises.
- **Objection defused:** "is this a subscription?"
- **Facts:** $89.00 GST-free (`01-source-map.md` §C and its §D flag).
- **Carrier:** PriceCard
- **Fails if:** it implies a purchase path that does not work from this site. **The `#enrol` anchor
  is a cutover consequence** (`01-source-map.md` §E) and the microcopy must not promise a payment
  method — the exact defect fixed on two live pages on 8 Aug.

### Section 6 — Who made it
`id="content-review"` · marker 06
- **Claim:** developed by ABE Education, from the same people who wrote the state courses.
- **Reader arrives:** last check before buying.
- **Facts:** Dominic Ogburn developed it; Warwick Smith reviews. **But note:** with no regulatory
  content, Warwick's "regulatory currency" framing barely applies. Say what is true — the tools are
  ABE Education's own work — rather than importing a review claim that does not fit.
- **Carrier:** Credentials
- **Fails if:** it borrows the courses' authority language for a product that has none.

## Forbidden carry-overs
- **No regulatory claim of any kind.** No "required", no "approved", no regulator named as endorsing
  it. It is voluntary.
- **No course language.** Not a course, no certificate, no completion, no enrolment in the training
  sense.
- **No "free" bait.** Stage 2 explicitly rules out chasing those queries; the page must not open with
  a free-template comparison as a hook.
- **No banned CTA**, no "comprehensive", no em dashes in body copy, "ABE Education" never bare.
