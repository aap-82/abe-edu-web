## Is this a real White Card? (`#real`, marker 01)

**Answer capsule** (50 words)

> Yes. The training is delivered and assessed by AlertForce, RTO 91826, and you receive a nationally
> recognised Statement of Attainment for CPCWHS1001, Prepare to work safely in the construction
> industry. AlertForce issues the Statement of Attainment; ABE Education publishes and supports the
> course and is not a Registered Training Organisation.

The White Card market has known bad actors, so check the provider before you pay, not after.
AlertForce holds CPCWHS1001 on its scope of registration and is approved to deliver and assess it in
the ACT.

You can confirm that yourself on training.gov.au, the national register, by searching RTO 91826. We
would rather you did. That is the difference between a real White Card and a worthless certificate:
the unit code and the RTO number, both verifiable, before any money changes hands.

## Will it be accepted, and can I do it online? (`#accepted`, marker 02)

**Answer capsule** (57 words)

> Yes to the first, no to the second. A White Card is nationally recognised the moment it's issued,
> accepted on every construction site in Australia. But in the ACT, this course is delivered
> face-to-face in a classroom by AlertForce, not online. That's AlertForce's own arrangement, not a
> WorkSafe ACT requirement, and other providers may offer it differently.

Most sites will not let you start work without a White Card, so the first doubt is settled quickly:
once issued, it is accepted everywhere, however the training was delivered. The second doubt is the
one worth being direct about. Some providers advertise an online ACT White Card. AlertForce, ABE
Education's RTO partner for the ACT, delivers this course face-to-face in a classroom instead.
Nothing in the WHS Regulation 2011 (ACT) requires that. It is simply how AlertForce runs it here,
the same as any RTO's own choice of delivery format.

## What do you get, and how do you get your card? (`#your-card`, marker 03)

**Answer capsule** (46 words)

> Passing the course gives you a Statement of Attainment, not the physical card. To get the card you
> apply online through an ACT Digital Account within 60 days, and Access Canberra posts it once
> approved, usually within about two weeks. There's no expiry once it's issued.

This is the step most providers do not mention, and the one most likely to catch you out. In the ACT
the card is not issued by the course provider. AlertForce gives you the Statement of Attainment; the
General Construction Induction Card itself comes from Access Canberra, and only after you apply.

Your receipt and training certificate let you start work while the application is assessed, which
Access Canberra typically completes within about a month. Miss the 60-day window and you will need to
arrange a fresh declaration from AlertForce confirming you completed the training, so applying in the
first week is the simplest path.

## What does an ACT White Card cost in total? (`#cost`, marker 04)

**Answer capsule** (41 words)

> The course is $137, paid to ABE Education. Applying for your card costs $47.00 through Access
> Canberra ($44.00 if you're replacing one), paid separately when you apply. That's $184.00 in total
> for a new card, and there's nothing else to pay.

The $47.00 application fee is the same regardless of how quickly you apply, provided you stay inside
the 60-day window, and is reviewed by Access Canberra each 1 July, so confirm the current amount
before you apply. There is no membership, renewal or annual charge — once your card is issued, it
does not expire.

AlertForce runs the training and the assessment together, in one classroom session. The format

enrol, since ABE Education is not the party running the session.

## FAQ (`#faq`, marker 07)

1. **Which state's White Card course do I need?**
   The one for the state where you'll be working, not where you live. If you live in New South
   Wales and are working on a site in the ACT, you need the ACT course.
2. **Can I do this course online?**
   No. In the ACT, AlertForce delivers this course face-to-face in a classroom. That's AlertForce's
   own arrangement, not a rule set by WorkSafe ACT — other providers may offer an online option, but
   ABE's ACT course does not.
3. **How long does it take to get my card?**
   Passing the course gives you a Statement of Attainment straight away. From there, you apply
   online via an ACT Digital Account within 60 days, and Access Canberra typically processes it
   within about a month before posting your card roughly two weeks after approval.
4. **Does the card expire?**
   No. Once issued, an ACT General Construction Induction Card does not expire.
5. **Is ABE Education a registered training organisation?**
   No. The course is delivered and assessed by AlertForce, RTO 91826. ABE Education publishes the
   course and enrols you; AlertForce delivers it and issues your nationally recognised Statement of
   Attainment.
6. **Is my ACT White Card valid in other states?**
   Yes. The card is nationally recognised the moment it's issued and accepted on construction sites
   across Australia, wherever you completed the training.
7. **What if I lose my card?**
   You can apply for a replacement through Access Canberra for $44.00.
