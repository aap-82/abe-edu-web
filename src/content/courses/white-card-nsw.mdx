<Section id="real" marker="01" eyebrow="Is it real?" h2="Is this a real White Card?">
  <AnswerCapsule>Yes. The training is delivered and assessed by Upskill Institute, RTO 45708, and you receive a nationally recognised Statement of Attainment for CPCWHS1001. In New South Wales an RTO needs two things to run this course: registration with ASQA, and a separate agreement with SafeWork NSW. Upskill Institute holds both.</AnswerCapsule>
  <div class="measure">
    <p>Most providers show you an RTO number. That number is worth checking, and it is worth knowing what it does not tell you on its own. Registration with ASQA lets an organisation deliver nationally recognised training. It does not, by itself, let anyone deliver construction induction training in New South Wales. SafeWork NSW requires a separate agreement, and its position is plain: only RTOs accredited by ASQA with an agreement from SafeWork NSW can deliver this training in the state.</p>
    <p>Upskill Institute Pty Ltd is RTO 45708, registered until July 2030, with CPCWHS1001 on its scope to both deliver and assess. It also holds SafeWork NSW registration RTO800520 for General Construction Induction Training. Both are public, and you can confirm them yourself before you pay us anything: the national register at training.gov.au for the first, and the SafeWork NSW register at verify.licence.nsw.gov.au for the second. We would rather you did.</p>
    <p>Upskill Institute issues your Statement of Attainment. ABE Education publishes this course, enrols you and supports you through it, and is not a registered training organisation.</p>

<Section bg="bg-alt" id="online" marker="02" eyebrow="Online in NSW" h2="Can you do the NSW White Card online?">
  <AnswerCapsule>Yes, entirely online, with no travel and no classroom. What you cannot do in New South Wales is complete it at your own pace. SafeWork NSW requires a live trainer in real time, so the course runs as a booked session on video rather than as a course you click through whenever it suits you.</AnswerCapsule>
  <div class="measure">
    <p>If you have been comparing prices, you have seen White Cards advertised for around forty dollars, finished the same hour, no appointment. Those are real courses. They are also not available to you as a New South Wales resident, and the reason is worth two minutes of your time.</p>
    <p>Fully self-paced online White Card training is permitted in Western Australia and Tasmania. Everywhere else, including New South Wales, the training has to happen with a live trainer, and SafeWork NSW sets a hard limit on what that delivery can contain. In its conditions for training organisations it states that connected delivery "must not include any pre-training, self-paced learning; or pre-recorded trainer videos or teaching course content".</p>
    <p>Upskill Institute is registered with SafeWork NSW to deliver general construction induction training, registration RTO800520, and it runs this course as a live trainer-led session under that registration. That is what lets you do it from your kitchen table without travelling to a classroom.</p>
    <p>So a trainer runs the session live, verifies who you are, watches you demonstrate the practical components and assesses you by direct observation. That is the requirement, not a service level ABE Education invented.</p>
  </div>

<Section id="accepted" marker="03" eyebrow="Where accepted" h2="Where is a NSW White Card accepted?">
  <AnswerCapsule>On construction sites anywhere in Australia. CPCWHS1001 is a nationally recognised unit, so the card you earn in New South Wales travels with you interstate. It works the other way too: SafeWork NSW accepts construction induction cards issued by other states and territories on NSW sites.</AnswerCapsule>
  <div class="measure">
    <p>If you already hold a White Card from Queensland, Western Australia or anywhere else in Australia, you probably do not need to buy this course. SafeWork NSW says construction workers can work in New South Wales using construction induction cards issued by other states and territories. Check your card is still valid, and if it is, keep your money.</p>
    <p>There is one condition that catches people out, and it has nothing to do with which state issued the card. A construction induction card becomes void if you have not carried out construction work for two consecutive years or more. If you have been out of the industry that long, the card in your wallet is no longer doing anything, wherever it came from, and you will need to complete the training again.</p>
  </div>
  <Note variant="caution" label="Two-year rule">Been out of construction for two years or more? Your existing card is void, whichever state issued it, and you will need to do the training again before you return to site.</Note>

<Section id="how-it-works" marker="06" eyebrow="How it works" h2="How does the session run?">
  <AnswerCapsule>One booked day with a live trainer, about seven hours including breaks, run over video. Sessions run Monday to Saturday from 9am to 4pm, with an evening class on Tuesdays from 4pm to 11pm. You attend, take part, complete the practical demonstrations and are assessed as you go.</AnswerCapsule>
  <div class="measure">
    <p>Because a trainer runs the session in real time, you book a day rather than starting whenever you like. Sessions run six days a week, and the Tuesday evening class exists for people who cannot lose a day's pay to get a ticket they need in order to earn it.</p>
    <p>Four things have to be sorted before the session starts, and every one of them stops the day if it is not. You need a Unique Student Identifier. You need a hundred points of identification. You need to be physically located in New South Wales while you are being assessed. And you need your own personal protective equipment in front of you, because part of the assessment is putting it on correctly while the trainer watches: a hi-vis vest or shirt, a hard hat, ear plugs and safety glasses.</p>
    <p>The assessment is conducted in English and you cannot be helped through it by anyone else.</p>
  </div>
