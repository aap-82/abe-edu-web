<Section bg="bg-alt" id="need-one" marker="02" eyebrow="Who needs one" h2="Do you actually need a White Card in Western Australia?">
  <AnswerCapsule>Yes, if you do construction work in Western Australia. The catch is that the regulations define construction work far more broadly than most people expect: maintenance, fit-outs, repairs, excavation and even landscaping done as site preparation all count. If you hold a current interstate card, you do not need to do this again.</AnswerCapsule>
  <CanCant
    canTitle="This counts as construction work"
    can={['Building, altering, converting or fitting out a structure.', 'Renovation, repair, maintenance and refurbishment.', 'Demolition, decommissioning and dismantling.', 'Excavation, and site preparation including landscaping.', 'Installing, testing or maintaining an essential service.']}
    cantTitle="You may not need this course"
    cant={['You already hold a current construction induction card from another state or territory.   />
  <div class="measure">
    <p>The definition sits at regulation 289 of the Work Health and Safety (General) Regulations 2022, and the breadth is deliberate. Landscaping is the one that catches people out: done as preparation for a build, it is construction work.</p>
    <p>Where it is genuinely marginal, the regulations ask whoever controls the workplace to weigh how complex the tasks are, how closely the person is supervised, and whether the construction work is incidental to the main business. The regulator’s own example is a farmer building an access road on their own property.</p>
    <p>If you are applying for a Western Australian owner builder approval, a white or blue card is one part of the knowledge pathway on Form 75, alongside a <a href="/wa-owner-builder-course">Western Australian owner builder course</a> completed within the last two years.</p>

<Section id="online" marker="03" eyebrow="Online in WA" h2="Can you do your White Card online in Western Australia?">
  <AnswerCapsule>Yes. Western Australia permits fully self-paced online White Card training, and the condition is specific: you must be able to show you were located in Western Australia at the time you sat the assessment. Being a Western Australian resident is not the test.</AnswerCapsule>
  <div class="measure">
    <p>WorkSafe WA supplies construction induction cards to training organisations on the condition that they are issued only to candidates who can prove that. Any one of these does it:</p>
    <ul>
      <li>a current Western Australian driver’s licence</li>
      <li>a current Western Australian learner driver’s permit</li>
      <li>current Western Australian student identification</li>
      <li>a recent utilities invoice showing a Western Australian address in your name</li>
      <li>Western Australian Construction Industry Training Fund payment eligibility</li>
      <li>a Western Australian postal address for the card to be sent to</li>
    </ul>
    <p>The distinction between location and residency matters more than it sounds. Someone who lives in Perth but sits the assessment while away working interstate does not meet the condition. Someone working in Western Australia on a short contract does.</p>
    <p>Where you are within the state makes no difference. The course runs the same from Perth as it does from Bunbury, Geraldton or a camp in the Pilbara, and because the assessment is a video call there is no classroom to drive to. That is the practical reason online delivery matters more in Western Australia than almost anywhere else.</p>
  </div>
  <VerifiedSources date="28 Jul 2026" facts="The evidence requirement and the accepted document list, from the regulator’s card-supply terms and conditions" sources={[{ label: 'WorkSafe WA — Candidates to provide evidence they are located in Western Australia', href: 'https://www.worksafe.wa.gov.au/candidates-provide-evidence-they-are-located-western-australia' }]} />

  <AnswerCapsule>Two parts. You work through the theory online at your own pace, which most Western Australian learners finish in two to six hours. Then you meet a Blue Dog Training assessor on a video call for fifteen to thirty minutes and demonstrate the practical safety tasks, including fitting and wearing the protective equipment.</AnswerCapsule>
  <div class="measure">
    <p>The video call is what separates this from a cheaper course, and it is worth understanding why it is there. The practical demonstration has been part of White Card assessment since December 2016, and regulators want it watched in real time rather than self-marked. An assessor sees you fit the equipment, corrects you if you have it wrong, and signs it off.</p>
    <p>It also means there is someone there when you are stuck. You book the call to suit yourself once the theory is done.</p>
    <p>Cheaper self-paced courses are lawful in Western Australia and the cards they issue are real. The credential is the same nationally recognised unit either way. What you are paying the difference for is a person on the other end of the call.</p>
  </div>
  <Note variant="caution" label="Before the call">You will need a device with a working camera and microphone, and the safety equipment you are asked to have ready for the demonstration.</Note>

<Section id="your-card" marker="06" eyebrow="Your card" h2="What do you get, and how long does it last?">
  <AnswerCapsule>A nationally recognised Statement of Attainment for CPCWHS1001, issued by Blue Dog Training, and a construction induction card posted to your Western Australian address. The card carries no expiry date and there is no refresher to sit. It only lapses if you leave the construction industry for two consecutive years or longer.</AnswerCapsule>
  <FactGrid items={[

  <div class="measure">
    <p>If your card is blue rather than white, it is still valid. WorkSafe WA changed the design in 2009 when the training was harmonised across the country, and both colours are accepted everywhere in Australia as evidence that you have completed the induction.</p>
    <p>If you have lost yours, WorkSafe WA runs a public database of cardholders. Look yourself up to find the training organisation that issued your card, then contact them for a replacement. If that organisation has since closed, another one may re-issue against your Statement of Attainment, at its own fee.</p>
