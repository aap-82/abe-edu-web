<Section id="your-year" marker="01" total="06" eyebrow="Your obligation" h2="Does eleven points cover your whole year?">
  <AnswerCapsule>No, and that is worth knowing before you buy. A Tasmanian electrician needs twelve CPD points a year and this bundle carries eleven, so one point has to come from elsewhere. Any CBOS-approved activity closes the gap, and the options are set out below.</AnswerCapsule>

  <FactGrid items={frontmatter.requirementRows} />

  <p>The requirement for an electrician comes from a different instrument to the one that governs builders and designers. Electricians, lineworkers, cable jointers and gas-fitters sit under the Occupational Licensing (Continuing Professional Development) Determination, which sets twelve points a year and phases a three-year licence to thirty-six across the full licence period. Thirty-six is that three-year total, not a figure for any single year.</p>

  <p>If you hold more than one occupational licence, you meet the highest single requirement rather than the sum. Some of that learning should still relate to each type of work you are licensed for.</p>

  <Note variant="note" label="Closing the last point">One point is a single approved online course, or an hour at a WorkSafe Tasmania session, or an industry association briefing. The one route that will not close it quickly is reading trade journals, which CBOS caps at three points a year.</Note>

<Section id="what-counts" bg="bg-warm" marker="02" total="06" eyebrow="What counts" h2="Do all eleven points actually count?">
  <AnswerCapsule>Yes. CBOS caps some ways of earning points, not some subjects: attending WorkSafe Tasmania events is capped at six a year, trade journals at three. Approved online courses carry no annual cap, and health and safety is a recognised CPD category, so all eleven of these count.</AnswerCapsule>

  <p>The distinction matters when you are topping up a short year. CBOS limits what you can claim for the things practitioners do incidentally: six points a year for WorkSafe Tasmania sessions, four for research, three for reading trade journals, two for association membership. Those limits attach to the activity, not to the topic.</p>

  <p>Course points are not limited that way. An approved online course earns a point per hour whatever its subject, so a bundle weighted toward safety counts exactly the same as one weighted toward technical content. What does matter is that each course is approved in its own right, which is why the table above carries a date for every one.</p>

  <Note variant="note" label="Where the caps do bite">If you were planning to make up a shortfall by reading journals or attending WorkSafe Month, those routes run out quickly: three and six points a year respectively. Course points do not.</Note>

  <VerifiedSources date="23 July 2026" facts="which CPD activities carry annual caps, and that approved online courses do not" sources={cbosSource} />
</Section>
